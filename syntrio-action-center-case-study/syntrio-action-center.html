Risk Action Center: Turning Incident Signals into Preventive Compliance Action

Dashboard that brought incident oversight, organisational trends, active case work, human-reviewed training and policy guidance into one compliance workspace.

  • Risk Action Center · enterprise desktop web application
  • Product Designer · concept and prototype contributor
  • Working users: compliance managers, incident managers, training administrators and policy stakeholders
  • Scope: incident oversight, trends, recommendations, recent activity and preventive-action framing
Problem Users Success criteria Workflow Decisions Delivery

Product opportunity

Incident data showed what had happened but not always what the organisation should do next

Compliance teams could capture and investigate workplace incidents, but the broader work of prevention could remain split across case management, reporting, employee training and policy-management processes.

A compliance manager who noticed a recurring concern might need to export incident information, analyse patterns outside the product, identify the affected audience, locate relevant training, contact the appropriate policy owner and document why the intervention was initiated.

The Action Center concept explored a more connected journey from detection to prevention: one workspace for current incident operations, organisational signals and relevant preventive guidance.

Design challenge

How might we help compliance managers move from reactive incident monitoring to timely, evidence-based preventive action while protecting sensitive report data and keeping every recommendation under human control?

Fragmented workflows

Incident, training and policy activities could require separate tools and manual handoffs.

Manual interpretation

Patterns could require exports and analysis outside the product before a response was coordinated.

Connected-platform opportunity

A unified feedback loop could connect detected concerns with relevant training and policy action.

Users

Start with the person coordinating prevention

Primary user

Compliance Programme Manager

Identify what needs attention, assess whether wider patterns justify prevention and coordinate an appropriate response while protecting sensitive data.

Primary user need

When I review incident activity, I need to identify what requires immediate attention and whether broader patterns justify preventive action, so I can coordinate the right response without exporting sensitive data or switching between disconnected systems.

  • Case Owner or Investigator Continues individual case follow-up.
  • Training Administrator Reviews learning content and audience.
  • Policy Owner, Legal or HR Reviews guidance and policy changes.
  • Compliance Leader Oversees ownership, risk and response.

Success criteria

What this concept should help people do

These are proposed criteria for evaluating the concept, not measured results. Numeric targets would follow a baseline with representative users.

Prioritise current work

Identify an urgent overdue or high-risk incident, understand why it needs attention and find its owner.

Understand the information

Distinguish current case status from historical trends and interpret reporting periods, risk, priority and scope correctly.

Assess suggested guidance

Explain why a training or policy resource appeared, identify its approved source and choose an appropriate next step.

Keep action accountable

Confirm the action’s scope, owner and due date while protecting sensitive report information and keeping decisions with people.

How the Action Center works

A clear path from signal to accountable response

The solution groups the proposed journey into three stages: notice a relevant pattern, let a person assess it, then coordinate and monitor an appropriate response.

01

Observe

Find a relevant signal.

  1. Incident reportsPermitted case and programme data
  2. Aggregated signalsPatterns within an authorised scope
  3. Trend or exceptionContext that may need review
02

Consider

Review possible responses.

  1. Suggested guidanceApproved training or policy resource
  2. Human reviewAccept, modify, dismiss or escalate
03

Act & learn

Assign and follow through.

  1. Preventive actionOwner, scope and due date
  2. Audit and monitoringDecision, status and future signals

Design decisions

Four decisions shaped the product proposition

01

Connected compliance

Bring reactive and preventive work into one starting point

Incident management explained what had been reported, while training and policy management supported broader prevention. The concept connected both sides so users could move from awareness to the appropriate next decision.

Product question
How can a signal from current case work support an organisation-level response?
Design response
Combine current workload, historical context, ownership and preventive guidance in one Action Center.
Trade-off
A broad dashboard risks overload, so immediate operational work and supporting analysis need a clear hierarchy.
02

Data semantics

Separate current operational snapshots from historical reporting

The original screen combines a six-month filter, a 30-day report count, month-over-month changes, current overdue incidents, current high-risk incidents and average resolution time.

Period-based reporting
New reports, resolved cases, trends and resolution-time measures for a defined date range.
Current-state snapshots
Active overdue, high-risk, unassigned or review-needed incidents as of a stated time.
Why it matters
Changing a reporting period should never hide an older unresolved incident from the active backlog.
What I would refine before validation

Show exact date ranges, last-updated time, metric definitions, comparison basis and direct drill-downs to the matching records.

03

Operational clarity

Keep risk, priority, status and ownership distinct

These fields answer different questions during triage. Combining them into one generic severity indicator would weaken accountability and make sorting less meaningful.

  • Risk: potential significance or exposure
  • Priority: operational urgency
  • Status: current workflow stage
  • Owner: person responsible for the next authorised action

These definitions are the conceptual model used to critique the design; the original product rules and permission model were not recovered.

04

Human-reviewed guidance

Keep recommendations explainable, advisory and under human control

The dashboard included AI-labelled training and policy suggestions, but it did not visibly explain their basis, source, time period, affected audience or available user action. AI suggests relevant training or policy guidance and explains why it made the suggestion which helps user to review and take appropriate actions.

Recommendation anatomy
Resource, affected scope, “Why suggested,” aggregate signal, data period, owner, version and review state.
User controls
Review evidence, accept, modify, dismiss, mark as addressed, assign an owner and give relevance feedback.
Critical guardrail
A suggestion must not determine fault, change case risk automatically, close an incident, assign mandatory training or update a policy without authorised review.
