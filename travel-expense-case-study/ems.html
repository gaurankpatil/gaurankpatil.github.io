Reframing expense approvals around risk, urgency, and action

A focused redesign of an enterprise Travel & Expense approval dashboard, helping managers identify requests needing attention, understand policy exceptions and process routine approvals.

  • Enterprise Travel & Expense Management Platform (Web)
  • Product Designer & Front-end Contributor
  • Approximately 2 weeks of design, reviews, iteration and front-end contribution
  • Figma, HTML5, Bootstrap, CSS and JavaScript
  • PM, engineers, finance stakeholders and client-side approvers
Problem Insights Workflow Success criteria Decisions Evaluation Delivery

Context and problem

Approvers did not need more data, but better prioritisation.

The approval dashboard contained the information managers needed, but approvers still had to work out what needed attention first.

The broader product was seeing lower-than-expected customer traction, and recurring feedback highlighted the approval experience as one area worth improving. Routine reports, ageing submissions, and policy exceptions could appear similar during scanning, Approvers described opening individual records and spending too much time to understand which requests needed attention, particularly when older submissions and policy exceptions appeared alongside routine work.

The goal was to improve the decision experience without rebuilding the core system.

Design challenge / Client feedback

How might we help approvers identify what requires attention, understand why it requires attention and take the appropriate action without opening every report?

Discovery and insight

Research focused on how people made approval decisions

Communicated with a small set of client-side expense approvers in managerial and finance roles and reviewed how they identified pending work, interpreted exceptions, and decided what required attention first and the entire user journey.

Combined those conversations with workflow walkthroughs, recurring client feedback, support issues and an assessment of the existing experience.

Approvers needed a clearer starting point for deciding what to review.

Evidence

Context hidden in individual records

Approvers described opening individual records to gather the context needed for a decision.

Design response

Move category, age, amount, policy state, and action earlier in the hierarchy.

Evidence

Counts shows volume but not urgency

Pending volume alone did not reveal how long work expenses had been waiting.

Design response

Show ageing bands for 0–7, 8–14, and 15+ days.

Evidence

Different types of risk

Ageing and policy exceptions represented different reasons to act.

Design response

Separate policy state from ordinary ageing status.

Evidence

Repetitive routine work

Eligible reports were processed one at a time even when risk was low.

Design response

Introduce page-level bulk approval with explicit safeguards.

Approval workflow

How the approval workflow changed

Previously, approvers opened each expense report to gather the details needed for a decision. The redesign brings decision context into the dashboard and supports a separate route for eligible bulk approvals.

Before

Review one report at a time

  1. 01
    Log in

    Enter the application.

  2. 02
    Arrive at the old dashboard

    See category cards and pending expenses.

  3. 03
    Open each report

    Read the details needed to make a decision.

  4. 04
    Approve or reject

    Decide after reviewing that report.

Repeat the report review and decision for each pending expense.

Redesigned flow

Start with context, then choose a review path

  1. 01
    Log in

    Enter the application.

  2. 02
    Arrive at the redesigned dashboard

    See category cards and report summaries.

  3. 03
    Review summaries

    Check the amount, pending duration and policy state.

Choose the appropriate review path

Review individually

  1. Review one request

    Use the amount, age and policy context in the summary.

  2. Open details when needed

    Inspect the report when it requires closer review.

  3. Approve or reject

    Make the decision after review.

Approve eligible requests together

  1. Open the bulk-approval view

    Use the View All / Bulk Approval entry point.

  2. Select eligible requests

    Limit selection to the current page.

  3. Confirm approval

    Review the selected requests before confirming.

Intended success criteria

What success should look like

Clearer Prioritisation

  • Task: Identify the highest-priority voucher.
  • Measure: Correct identification, time taken and navigation path.

Correct policy interpretation

  • Task: Compare an older request without an exception with a newer request containing one.
  • Measure: Interpretation of each status and the chosen next action.

Risk comprehension

  • Task: Distinguish policy violations from ordinary ageing.
  • Measure: Understand why exception-heavy vouchers require individual review.

Workflow efficiency

  • Task: Select eligible requests from the current page.
  • Measure: Reduce unnecessary visits to individual detail pages for routine work

Understand the result

  • Task: Respond to a failed or partially completed approval action.
  • Measure: Understanding of what succeeded, what failed, and how to recover.

Design decisions

Three decisions shaped the new approval experience

Prioritisation

Make ageing visible before detailed review

A pending count tells an approver how much work exists, but not which work is becoming urgent. I introduced ageing distribution within each report category so approvers could distinguish recent submissions from reports waiting longer.

  • 0–7 days for recent reports
  • 8–14 days for items needing attention
  • 15+ days as the highest-priority ageing group
What I would improve today

Make every band label and count explicit, and reassess whether a compact stacked bar would support comparison better than repeated donut charts.

Risk semantics

Separate policy risk from ordinary ageing

A report could require attention because it was old, because it violated policy or because both were true. I surfaced policy state directly in the report summary. This was intended to help approvers understand the reason for attention before choosing the next action.

Critical safeguard: Reports containing policy violations were not eligible for bulk approval.

Efficiency with safeguards

Make routine approval faster, with explicit guardrails

Routine requests involved repeated approval actions. A wider selection could make it harder to understand which requests would be affected, especially when policy exceptions were present.

Introduced page-level selection for eligible reports. Policy-breach reports were excluded, approval confirmation and rejection-reason capture provided accountability around consequential actions. The action was limited to the current page

  • Select all applies to the current page
  • Ineligible reports remain visible but require individual review
  • Confirmation clarifies the action before completion

Evaluation

Evaluation plan and evidence status

The dashboard is now in client use. I did not conduct final usability testing. The checks below describe a proposed evaluation; verified task-performance results are not available for this case study.

Clearer prioritisation

Task: Choose the next request to review from a mixed queue.

Record: Correctness against agreed priority rules, time taken and unnecessary detail-page visits.

Correct policy interpretation

Task: Compare an older routine request with a newer request containing a policy exception.

Record: Explanation of each status and choice of review action.

Safe routine approvals

Task: Approve eligible requests from the current page while excluding policy exceptions.

Record: Correct completion, time, selection mistakes, understanding of scope and results, and recovery from a simulated partial failure.
